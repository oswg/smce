This may be one of the more slightly controversial messages from our friends in the Confederation, and yet I think it deserves more reflection before one lets discomfort become dismissal. In my view it is quite well that those of Hatonn dive directly into the language that strikes us as so "unspiritual" -- hunting, prey, _aggressiveness_ -- in order to disabuse the listener of the normal paens to peace and passivity that accompany these little sermonettes. Of course, I don't think the message is one against peace or pacivism; it only throws into stark relief the dynamics of seeking, recognizing the spiritual work involved in preparing oneself for the encounter with the love sought.

And yet, is there _not_ an element of passivity involved with the entire concept of patiently stalked one's prey? Yes and no, right? Is there not an active, striving element involved in the training of one's mind towards that single-minded focus to which to exhort us to turn? So I advise the reader to attempt not to simply push away the violent rhetoric.

<!-- more -->

The whole tone of this excerpt strikes me as those of Hatonn drawing upon the language of our mythologies and fables, which often teach the most sublime lessons through the most grotesque imagery. The Greek myths in particular seem to alert their students to the need to pay attention to a kind of cunning -- gods tricking humans, humans pulling one over on the gods -- that has deep correlations, in my view, to working with an illusion that is nevertheless shot through with the divine.

It is paradoxical beyond most paradox because it impinges directly upon the quality of our humans lives _as lived_. We all find ourselves paralyzed by ceaseless activity and incessant details. We all also find ourselved moved to exaltation and aliveness through witnessing, through meditation and reflection. These paradoxes are built into the way language, and by extension _consciousness_, yoke our minds to worldly concepts and the material ground of being. Yet it is through these distorting, confusing, sometimes downright deceitful words that we recognize their original inspirations, the vibrations that got distorted in our material experience such that, yes, they are perverted or muddied _but also made tangible and apprehendable_ such that we can slowly or suddenly be transformed by them.

This is where I think one can usefully apply the language of cunning, of aggression, of pursuit: to exert one's consciousness towards occupying a point of view in which the unity of these things can at least be thought at all. We are training ourselves to be co-Creators in third density, to reckon directly with the enormity of infinity and free ourselves from the illusion of finity. We need all of our hunter's tools just to make the occupation of that perspective thinkable at all, that greater self Hatonn speaks of imaginable in the first place, so that we understand our faith is both passive and active, and we can take more charge of it as we refine our will.

Please note all speakers in this excerpt are unknown, though it's obvious to me that Carla is at least one of them. I've also gotten rid of instrument transfers to make the central point clearer and smoother, and removed extraneous commentary about instruments.

> …as we have said, it is all too easy to look upon yourselves as passive receivers of spiritual food. If you do so look upon yourself, you will find your progress to be slow. > And what, then, are your weapons?
